Deep Dive

Your Own Data!

Estimated time to read: 10 minutes Depends on: Data Ingestion, Precomputed Tracking & QC, JABS Demo Audience: anyone with their own open-field-arena (OFA) recordings who wants to run them through this course's workflow, not just the example dataset

This page is not a notebook you run - it is a map. Everything else in this module works with one curated example dataset. This page is for when you are ready to point the same pipeline at your own mouse video data.

Learning Goals¶

By the end of this page, you should be able to:

  • Name the four stages of the official JABS pipeline (data acquisition, pose estimation, annotation and classifier training, genetic analysis) and which tool handles each
  • Recognize what your own open-field-arena recordings need to look like to work with this pipeline
  • Locate the Kumar Lab tools that turn raw video into JABS-compatible pose files
  • Map your own data onto the same ingestion -> QC -> annotation -> classification -> analysis workflow this course walks through
  • Know where to go for help if your data does not fit the expected format

Is This Page For You?¶

This page is for you if:

  • You have your own open-field-arena video of mice (or a similar single-animal, top-down recording setup)
  • You want to annotate and classify a behavior using JABS, the way this course demonstrates
  • You do not yet have pose-estimation keypoints for your video

If you already have JABS-format pose files (*_pose_est_v6.h5), skip ahead to Precomputed Tracking & QC - the same code runs on your files wherever this course's pages point at data/.

The Official JABS Pipeline¶

JABS is published as a four-stage, end-to-end pipeline, each stage handled by its own tool (Choudhary, A., Geuther, B. Q., Sproule, T. J., Beane, G., Kohar, V., Trapszo, J., & Kumar, V., "JAX Animal Behavior System (JABS), a genetics-informed, end-to-end advanced behavioral phenotyping platform for the laboratory mouse," eLife 107259 (2026), elifesciences.org/articles/107259):

Stage Module Tool
Data acquisition JABS-DA JABS-data-pipeline
Pose estimation - mouse-tracking-runtime
Behavioral annotation & classifier training JABS-AL JABS-behavior-classifier
Genetic analysis JABS-AI (deploys trained classifiers across large genetic cohorts - not covered by this course)

Steps 1-4 below walk you through the first three stages using your own data. The fourth stage, JABS-AI, is about running a classifier across a large-scale genetic dataset to find genetic drivers of behavior - a different scale of problem than this course's own Results and Analysis page, which evaluates a single classifier against ground truth. If you get as far as wanting to do genetics-scale analysis, JABS-AI is where that work lives, but it is out of scope for this page.

Step 1: Check Your Recording Setup¶

This is the data acquisition stage (JABS-DA) - the JABS-data-pipeline repo has the hardware build guide if you are setting up a new rig from scratch.

Arena, lighting, and camera (Geuther, B.Q., Deats, S.P., Fox, K.J., Murray, S.A., Braun, R.E., White, J.K., Chesler, E.J., Lutz, C.M., Kumar, V. "Robust mouse tracking in complex environments using neural networks." Communications Biology 2, 124 (2019). doi:10.1038/s42003-019-0362-1):

  • 52 cm x 52 cm x 23 cm arena - white PVC plastic floor, gray PVC plastic walls, with a 2.54 cm chamfer on all inner edges (aids cleaning)
  • LED ring light (F&V R300), calibrated to 600 lux
  • Sentech STC-MB33USB camera with a Computar T3Z2910CS-IR lens, mounted about 100 cm above the arena, zoomed to 8 px/cm
  • 640x480 px, 8-bit monochrome, 30 fps (cropped to 480x480 px for analysis)
  • Single animal, top-down view only - the pipeline is not validated for multiple animals in frame
  • A subset of arenas are set up for continuous 24-hour monitoring on a 12:12 light-dark cycle: 250 lux during the day, under 5 lux at night, with additional 940 nm IR LED lighting for night recording

This same arena and the cropped 480x480 px @ 30 fps format are reused in later Kumar Lab publications, e.g. Sheppard, K., Gardin, J., Sabnis, G.S., Peer, A., Darrell, M., Deats, S., Geuther, B., Lutz, C.M., Kumar, V. "Stride-level analysis of mouse open field behavior using deep-learning-based pose estimation." Cell Reports 38, 110231 (2022). doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2021.110231.

Your video needs to reasonably match this setup for the pose model to produce usable keypoints; a substantially different camera angle, arena size, or lighting will degrade pose quality in ways the Precomputed Tracking & QC page's checks are designed to catch, not prevent.

Step 2: Generate Pose Estimates from Your Video¶

This is the pose estimation stage. Your video needs to become a JABS-format pose file before anything else in this course applies. The Kumar Lab's mouse-tracking-runtime pipeline does exactly this: it runs a trained pose-estimation model on your video and writes out a poseest/ HDF5 file with points and confidence in the same 12-keypoint schema used throughout this course.

  • For the schema itself (what points and confidence mean, and why the keypoints are ordered the way they are), see Data Ingestion.
  • For how a confidence map actually becomes a keypoint and a confidence value, see the Pose Estimation deep dive.

Follow the mouse-tracking-runtime repository's own setup instructions to run it against your video - that pipeline, not this course, is where a pose model actually gets applied to new data.

Step 3: Quality-check Your Pose Files¶

Once you have pose files for your own sessions, run them through the same QC this course applies to its example dataset. Precomputed Tracking & QC auto-detects whichever data/ subfolder you point it at (data_subdir) - drop your own pose files into a folder there and the same thresholds, histograms, and per-keypoint reliability checks run unchanged. Pay particular attention to the per-keypoint reliability chart: a keypoint that is unreliable on your setup but fine on the course's example data is a sign your recording setup differs from what the pose model expects.

Step 4: Annotate, Train, and Classify¶

This is the behavioral annotation and classifier training stage - JABS-AL. With QC-passed sessions, follow JABS Demo to open your data in the JABS GUI, label a behavior, train a classifier, and run it - the same walkthrough this course uses for its own example behavior (grooming) applies to whatever behavior you choose to label in your data.

Step 5: Analyze Your Results¶

Once you have classifier predictions on your own sessions, Results and Analysis walks through turning them into ethograms, bout-duration distributions, and classifier evaluation metrics - again, the same code, run on your own predictions instead of the course's example set.

Contact¶

If your data does not fit the setup above, or you run into trouble applying JABS to it, the Kumar Lab maintains a support address for exactly this: jabs@jax.org (from the JABS-behavior-classifier README). That same README notes that multi-mouse support is still under development - contact them directly if that is your use case.

The tool itself is developed and maintained at The Jackson Laboratory by the Kumar Lab.

What Next¶

This page only maps the path from your own video to a trained classifier - the actual pipeline runs happen outside this course, since they depend on your own hardware, video, and behavior of interest. If you get stuck at any single stage, the pages linked above go into far more depth on that stage than this page does.
